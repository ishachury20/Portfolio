Overview

Genesis is a community-based freight and moving platform designed to connect customers with independent transporters across Latin America, acting as a sustainable alternative to historically fragmented networks.

I owned and led the interface design for Genesis while the product was still undefined, translating an ambitious, largely unscoped vision into a usable, trustworthy platform — shaping the core user flows, the visual system, and the interactions that would hold the experience together as the product itself was still being figured out.

The final product vision evolved from an early concept into a complete, interactive booking experience supported by a robust component system, successfully establishing the foundation as the team prepared for development.

Context

The vision was massive, but the blueprint was a blank slate

Across Latin America, independent freight and moving services often operate through informal, fragmented networks — word of mouth, scattered listings, and little to no way for a customer to verify who they were actually trusting with their belongings. Genesis set out to formalize that network into a single platform, connecting customers directly with independent transporters.

There was no existing product to redesign and no prior research to build on — just an ambitious concept and a small team tasked with figuring out what the product actually needed to be, from the ground up.

The Problem

How do you introduce complex logistics without being overwhelming?

Genesis needed to help customers coordinate an unfamiliar logistical process while eventually supporting an ecosystem of independent drivers across different regions, devices, and levels of digital familiarity.

The service itself was inherently complex. A single booking could involve locations, cargo limitations, vehicle selections and extensive communication on both sides. Exposing all of that at once would risk overwhelming users before they completed their first booking.
